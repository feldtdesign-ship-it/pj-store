The story

No investors.No connections.No permission.

A kid from Tulsa, Oklahoma dropped out of college, landed on a friend's couch in Astoria, and gave himself one month to figure New York out. This is how a Home Depot cart became a brand.

2012
Chapter one · The cart

The subway was the first gallery. Raw, public, unforgiving.

On September 26, 2012, PJ wheeled a cart he had built from a single Home Depot run, wired together with metal clips and chicken wire, onto an MTA platform and started selling original prints to strangers between trains.

There was no plan B. He worked out of a basement studio apartment, drew on the train, and sold on the platform. For eight years, that was the whole business: the artist, the art, and whoever was waiting for the L.

Proving demand the hardest way there is.

2014
Chapter two · DUMBO

A used heat press and a box of plastisol transfers. That was the clothing line.

Pop-ups under the bridge in DUMBO moved the work off paper and onto fabric. Prints became tees. Tees became a following. The designs were the same ones that had already sold underground, so nothing was a guess.
